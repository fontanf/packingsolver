Tree search¶

Objectives¶

This algorithm solves the feasibility, knapsack, open-dimension-x, open-dimension-y, open-dimension-z, bin-packing and bin-packing-with-leftovers objectives.

Principle¶

It is a direct generalization of the tree search for rectangle to three dimensions: a single item is packed at each stage, either in the current (last) bin or in a new bin, and the tree is explored by an iterative beam search.

In the direction X, the items are packed along the x-axis. A node stores the skyline of its current bin: the list of the uncovered items, the parts of the packed items visible from the end of the x-axis, each described by its x-coordinate and its ranges along y and z. A new item, in one of its allowed rotations (up to 6), starts along y at the end of an uncovered item (or at 0), and along z at the end of an uncovered item (or at 0); it is then pushed along x until it doesn’t overlap the skyline. The directions Y and Z are the same with the axes exchanged.

The directions used depend on the instance: the open dimension or the axis of the leftover when there is one, all three directions in separate searches when there is a single bin type; otherwise, each new bin is filled along its longest dimension.

Dominance¶

As in two dimensions, two nodes with the same items packed are compared through their skylines. When the gap between an item and a side of the bin is too small for any remaining item, the skyline is considered as reaching the side there, which makes more nodes comparable.

Guides¶

The guides are the same as in two dimensions, with volumes instead of areas: by default, guides 4 and 5 (the volume lost divided by the profit) for the knapsack objective, and guides 0 and 1 (the volume lost divided by the volume of the items packed) for the other objectives.

Automatic selection¶

With a single bin, the tree search is run unless the objective is knapsack or feasibility and many items (more than 64 on average) fit in the bin, in which case the tree search with maximal spaces is run instead. With several bins, it is run when the item types have few copies compared to the number of items fitting in a bin, alongside the generic algorithms using it to solve their sub-problems. See algorithm selection.

It can be forced with --use-tree-search 1 (CLI) or use_tree_search (Python).
