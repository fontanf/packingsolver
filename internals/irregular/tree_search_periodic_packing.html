Tree search with periodic packings¶

Objectives¶

This algorithm only handles instances with a single bin. It solves the knapsack and feasibility objectives directly, and the bin-packing, bin-packing-with-leftovers, open-dimension-x, open-dimension-y and open-dimension-xy objectives through the sequential feasibility algorithm.

Principle¶

It is designed for instances with many copies of a few item types. Packing such items one by one, as the tree search does, requires many decisions which all look alike. Instead, the good ways to pack many copies of an item type together are computed first, as periodic packings, and the tree search packs whole blocks of them at once.

The tree search is the one of the tree search with maximal spaces for rectangle, where each block is described by its bounding box.

Periodic packings¶

A periodic packing is a lattice packing: a unit cell of one or two items, repeated by the translations \(n v_1 + m v_2\) for integers \(n\) and \(m\), where \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) are the two vectors of the lattice, without overlap between the copies.

For each item type and each of its orientations, the following unit cells are considered:

  • a single item;

  • two items: the item in orientation \(r\) and the item in orientation \(r + 180°\), when it is allowed. The candidate positions of the second item are the vertices of the no-fit polygon of the two items, where the second item touches the first one without overlapping it.

Given a unit cell, the region forbidden to the translations of the cell is the union of the no-fit polygons of its items with each other. The lattice vectors are then computed in two ways:

  • horizontal: \(v_1\) is the shortest horizontal translation outside the forbidden region; \(v_2\) is the lowest translation outside the forbidden regions of the cell and of its copy translated by \(v_1\);

  • vertical: the same with the roles of the x and y axes exchanged.

A rectangular lattice, where \(v_2\) is perpendicular to \(v_1\), is also considered in each case. Each candidate lattice is checked on the actual shapes.

Periodic packings mixing item types are also built, for the items shaped like an L or a U: such a wrapper item has a large empty rectangle touching a side of its bounding box. A base, a single item of another item type or the unit cell of one of its periodic packings, is put in this empty rectangle, and a second copy of the wrapper, rotated by 180°, is put on the other side of the base.

The periodic packings only depend on the item types, so they are computed once by the instance builder, on simplified shapes, and stored in the instance.

Blocks¶

The blocks are built from the periodic packings, for the bin of the instance:

  • the tilings of \(n_x \times n_y\) unit cells, as long as they fit in the bin and don’t use more copies than available;

  • the clipped lattice blocks: the bin is filled with the lattice, for several positions of the lattice, and a block is made of the items whose top and right are below given thresholds. Unlike the tilings, these blocks may contain partial unit cells, which is often denser along the sides of the bin;

  • the grid blocks: the items aligned on a grid of their bounding boxes, for the orientations without periodic packing;

  • the framed blocks: a block surrounded by one, two or four copies of a wrapper item, in rotations \(r\), \(r + 180°\), and \(r + 90°\) and \(r + 270°\). A framed block is only kept if it takes no more space than the block and the frame items packed separately. Framed blocks can be framed again once.

A block dominates another one if it contains the same item types, at least as many copies of each, and takes no more space in both dimensions. Dominated blocks are discarded as they are generated, except the blocks of a single item, which are always kept: they may be the only way to pack the last copy of an item type.

Branching scheme¶

A node stores the blocks placed and the list of the maximal empty spaces of the bin: the empty rectangles which aren’t contained in another empty rectangle. The obstacles (the borders of a non-rectangular bin, the defects and the fixed items) are approximated by rectangles and cut out of the empty spaces from the start.

To generate the children of a node, the empty space with a corner closest to a corner of the bin is selected, and one child is generated for each block fitting in it, placed in that corner. The empty spaces are then updated.

Each insertion is scored by

\[V \times C^\alpha \times F^\beta \times N^{-\gamma}\]

where \(V\) is the profit of the block, \(C\) its relative contact with the sides of the space and the other blocks, \(F\) its fill rate (the area of its items divided by the area of its bounding box), and \(N\) its number of items. Two sets of exponents are used: one while the bin is not filled much, and the other once the fill rate of the node exceeds a threshold. The children of a node are generated by decreasing score.

A node is evaluated by completing it greedily, inserting the best scored block at each step; the beam search keeps the nodes with the best completions. The final solutions are completed greedily too.

Search¶

The tree is explored by the iterative beam search generating the children one at a time, by decreasing score, with a growth factor of 2. Two nodes with the same number of copies of each item type packed are compared by their greedy values: the one with the smallest greedy value is dominated. Since the depth of a node is its number of blocks, the dominance compares the nodes of all the queues (global dominance). In the non-anytime modes, the search is run with a queue of size 1, then with a queue of the size given by the parameter not_anytime_tree_search_periodic_packing_queue_size (16 by default).

Automatic selection¶

With a single bin, the algorithm is run when the instance is dominated by item types with many copies (more than 16): when these items alone take more than 80% of the free area of the bin, or more than 80% of the area of all the items. The tree search is then run too if fewer than 64 items fit in the bin. See algorithm selection.

It can be forced with --use-tree-search-periodic-packing 1 (CLI) or use_tree_search_periodic_packing (Python).
